Think Before You Click

Random links and QR codes can lead to scams, malware, and stolen data

Why Random Links Are Dangerous

Phishing

Attackers create fake websites that look identical to real services and trick users into entering passwords or payment information.

Malware

Some links download malicious software onto your device which can steal files, record keystrokes, or spy on activity.

Account Takeover

Fake login pages collect usernames and passwords that criminals later use to access real accounts.

QR Codes Hide the Destination

A QR code hides the website address behind a simple square pattern. You cannot see the destination before scanning it. Criminals exploit this limitation to redirect people to malicious websites.

This attack technique is becoming more common in parking meters, restaurant menus, posters, and emails.

How Attackers Trick People

Cyber criminals often rely on urgency and curiosity. They create messages that make people react quickly without verifying the source.

Examples include fake package tracking links, fake login alerts, or QR codes promising rewards.

Common QR Code Scams

Parking meter scams

Fake QR stickers redirect drivers to payment pages that steal credit card details.

Fake restaurant menus

Malicious codes placed over real ones lead to phishing pages.

Public posters

Attackers replace legitimate QR codes with ones that download malware.

How to Protect Yourself

Verify the source

Only open links from trusted senders and official websites.

Check the URL

Look carefully at the domain name before entering any credentials.

Avoid random QR codes

Be cautious with codes placed in public areas or unexpected messages.
